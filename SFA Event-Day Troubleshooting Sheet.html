SFA Event-Day Troubleshooting Sheet

Prepared for event-day testing and operations. Updated April 22, 2026.

This sheet is for the Admin PWA check-in flow. It explains what you may see during offline, reconnect, websocket, auth, and waiver scenarios, what those signals mean, and what action to take next.

Quick Decision Guide

Green or recovered in a few seconds: expected Amber queue banner: safe to continue, waiting to sync Waiver block: fix waiver, then retry Stale other-device view for a few seconds: usually websocket or polling delay
  • If the amber queue clears after reconnect, the recovery worked.
  • If a second device catches up within about 5 to 10 seconds, treat that as normal fallback behavior.
  • If the queue does not clear after reconnect and a manual refresh, treat it as a real sync issue.
  • If the app sends you back to login, your token likely expired. Log in again and continue.

What the Dev Diagnostics Panel Means

  • Network: whether the browser currently thinks the device is online.
  • Pending queue: how many check-ins are stored locally waiting to reach the server.
  • Participant refresh: the last successful or failed fetch of event participants.
  • Queue source: what triggered the last queue retry, such as online-event, window-focus, or queue-interval.
  • WebSocket status: whether the live update connection is connecting, open, or reconnecting.

Scenario Reference

Offline or dropped network during check-in

You might see: a red offline message plus an amber queue banner saying check-ins are pending sync.

Meaning: the app kept the check-in locally and will retry automatically.

What to do: keep working if needed, restore connectivity, then leave the page open or bring it back into focus. The queue should retry on reconnect, focus, visibility, or the periodic retry loop.

Escalate if: the queue is still present 10 to 15 seconds after connectivity is clearly back and a manual refresh does not clear it.

Waiver not verified

You might see: a red message naming the participant and explaining that waiver verification is required.

Meaning: the server intentionally rejected the check-in. This is not an offline or sync bug.

What to do: verify the waiver in the admin workflow, then retry the check-in.

Escalate if: the participant is already verified and the same rejection continues.

Other device looks stale for a few seconds

You might see: phone updates first, laptop lags, or the reverse.

Meaning: websocket delivery may be reconnecting or delayed, and the polling fallback should catch up shortly.

What to do: wait a few seconds first. If it stays stale, tap Refresh once and confirm the backend is reachable.

Escalate if: both devices are online and visible, but updates keep missing after 5 to 10 seconds.

Backend unavailable

You might see: refresh attempts fail, websocket status moves to reconnecting, or check-ins queue instead of syncing.

Meaning: the frontend cannot currently reach the API or websocket endpoint.

What to do: check that the backend process is running, confirm the frontend is using the current laptop host and port 8000, then retry.

Escalate if: the API base looks correct but both refresh and queue retries keep failing.

Forced websocket interruption

You might see: websocket status changing to closed or warning, then back to connecting or open.

Meaning: live updates are temporarily down, but the app is attempting to reconnect automatically.

What to do: watch for the websocket state to recover. If the refresh panel keeps showing successful polling, data should still catch up.

Escalate if: websocket never returns to open and polling also stops refreshing participant data.

Login redirect during a long session

You might see: the app sends you back to the login screen.

Meaning: the token is expired or invalid. This is auth state, not a check-in sync failure.

What to do: log in again and reopen the event.

Escalate if: login immediately loops or tokens expire far faster than expected.
